The fear in buying land,
and what technology can do about it.

Land disputes are not only a legal problem. They are an information problem, and information problems can be engineered against.

Ask anyone in western Uganda about buying land and you will hear the same sentence: "You have to be careful." That sentence is doing a lot of work. It is carrying the cost of a system where the buyer holds almost all the risk.

What the buyer cannot see

  • Whether the seller actually owns the land, and whether anyone else has a claim on it
  • Where the boundary really runs, as opposed to where the seller says it runs
  • Whether the same plot has already been sold to someone else
  • Whether the money will move before or after verification

None of that is a legal mystery. It is missing information, delivered late and unevenly, which is exactly the kind of problem software is good at.

Three things technology can do

1. Make verification visible. A listing that carries a GPS-verified boundary and a document record turns a verbal claim into something a buyer can inspect before paying.

2. Put payment behind the evidence. Escrow changes the order of operations: money is committed, but released against milestones rather than against a promise.

3. Give the plot a history. A digital land passport, with boundaries, ownership history and documents attached, makes the next sale cheaper than this one, because the evidence already exists.

What technology cannot do

It cannot replace a title registry, a surveyor or a court. Anyone selling you a platform that claims to is selling you something else. What a platform can do is make the honest transaction easier than the dishonest one, which is the only durable way to change behaviour.

That is the design brief behind Sageco Evergreen: verified listings, GPS boundaries, escrow-protected payments and land passports in one place, so trust becomes a feature of the product rather than a favour from the seller.

The goal is not a clever platform. The goal is a family that does not lose its savings twice.
