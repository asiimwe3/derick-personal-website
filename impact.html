LEADERSHIP

How I lead

Leadership here means two companies running at once, with small teams and no layer to hide behind. It means setting direction, doing the work, and staying reachable after launch, because the person who wrote the code is also the person who answers the phone.

What that looks like in practice: plain communication, decisions made in the open, and a bias to shipping something working rather than describing something perfect. Mistakes are named early: on Sageco Evergreen, a database permission model that would have let a stranger delete real bookings was found and rebuilt before launch, not after.

What he is learning: delegation, hiring, and the discipline of saying no to work that does not serve the mission.

MENTORSHIP

Bringing people with me

Mentorship in western Uganda rarely looks like a programme. It looks like a young person with a laptop asking how to get their first client. Derick's approach is practical: show the work, share the numbers, and help with the specific next step rather than a motivational speech.

He is building tools that lower the entry cost for beginners, a voice-first editor for people who think faster than they type, and calculators that remove the maths barrier to trade work.

SOCIAL IMPACT

Impact that is measurable

Access

Software priced for small business

DeryCode builds systems that a shop in Kyenjojo can afford and run without an IT department. Affordable software is the difference between a business keeping records and losing them.

Protection

Land savings protected

Sageco Evergreen exists so a family can verify what they are buying before they hand over savings: GPS boundaries, land passports, escrow.

Skills

Digital skills in the region

Showing young people in Fort Portal and Kyenjojo that software work is available from home, not only in Kampala.

Local economy

Local businesses online

Hotels, clinics, hardware shops and homestays given a real web presence, which turns search traffic into customers for the local economy.

COMMUNITY DEVELOPMENT

Where I come from

Fort Portal and Kyenjojo are home. The businesses there are the first clients, the first case studies and the first beneficiaries: hotels, clinics, hardware shops, homestays and community organisations that needed a real web presence and could not afford an agency in Kampala.

He also builds for community organisations, including work for the Western Youth Tech Foundation and a free destination guide to Visit Fort Portal that sends visitors to local businesses.

YOUTH EMPOWERMENT

For the ones coming after

You do not need permission to build. You need a problem you care about and the discipline to finish something small.

The message he gives young people in western Uganda is the one his own record demonstrates: start before you are ready, ship something real, publish it, and let the work answer the questions about your age.
