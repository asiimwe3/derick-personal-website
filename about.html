BIOGRAPHY

Who I am

Asiimwe Derick is a social entrepreneur from Kyenjojo, in western Uganda, born on 9 January 2006. He is the founder of DeryCode Technologies, a software company building offline-first systems and websites for Ugandan businesses, and the CEO of Sageco Evergreen, a property platform built around verified listings, GPS boundaries and escrow-protected payments.

He is twenty years old and still completing school, running both companies in the hours that school leaves him. He states that constraint plainly because it shapes how he works: small teams, fast shipping, and systems designed for unreliable power, weak internet and small budgets, because those are the conditions he builds in.

His work sits at the meeting point of two problems he kept meeting personally: businesses that cannot afford or operate the software sold to them, and families who lose savings to land deals they cannot verify. Both are information problems before they are legal or technical ones, and both can be engineered against.

He writes in public, publishes his numbers, and is open to angel and strategic investors in both companies. His stated long-term ambition, Vision 2040, is to move from building companies to investing in the people building Africa's future.

MY STORY

How this started

It started with watching people work hard inside systems that were not built for them. In Kyenjojo, that looks like a shop owner who knows every item in his head because the software costs more than his margin, a family that has paid for land twice because the first proof was a paper in a drawer, a student with no record of what he has actually learned.

The first response was technical: learn to build, then build the thing that is missing. That is how DeryCode Technologies began, writing websites and business systems for local companies, then keeping them running long after launch, because a small business with no IT department cannot absorb a handover that ends in a broken invoice.

The second response was structural. Software alone does not fix a market where the buyer cannot verify what they are buying. That is why Sageco Evergreen exists: to put verification, boundaries and payment protection into the same place, so trust becomes a product feature rather than a favour.

He is honest that both companies are early. The point of publishing the story this way is that the work is checkable: live sites, live listings, real clients, real numbers.

IDENTITY & BRAND

What the name stands for

Name: Asiimwe Derick. Publicly, Derick. Positioning: a young Ugandan builder who ships and publishes his numbers, not a motivational speaker. Promise: tools that work where people actually are, and deals done on transparency.

Voice: plain, specific, unexaggerated. Short sentences. Numbers over adjectives. He would rather be trusted than impressive. Visual identity: warm amber on deep navy, Space Grotesk for headings, DM Sans for text, generous white space and photography that shows the work rather than stock imagery.

What he refuses: inflated claims, invented credentials, and the habit of selling African founders a dream instead of a product.

PHILOSOPHY & MANIFESTO

What I actually believe

Character before titles. The business changes; what you are does not.

1. Build where the need is greatest. The interesting problem is not in the market that already has eleven solutions. It is with the shop owner, the farmer and the family buying land.

2. Offline is a feature, not a limitation. Software that needs perfect conditions is software for someone else's country.

3. Price for the person you claim to serve. If the tool costs more than the problem, it is not a solution.

4. Publish the numbers. Claims should be checkable. Live links, real listings, real clients.

5. Support is part of the product. A launch is the middle of the job, not the end.

6. Open doors behind you. Every chance to pull someone up is part of the work, not charity around it.

7. Stay a student. Ask why before how, and keep asking after you are the one leading.
