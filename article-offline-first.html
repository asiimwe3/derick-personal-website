TECHNOLOGY · ESSAY

Why Ugandan software
has to work offline first.

Most software sold here assumes fast internet, a card and someone on staff to administer it. Most Ugandan businesses have none of those things. Designing for that reality is not a compromise, it is the product.

Walk into a shop in Kyenjojo and watch what happens when the internet drops. The till still works because it is a book. The stock still counts because it is in the owner's head. The business keeps running because it never depended on a connection in the first place.

Then watch the same shop try a cloud point-of-sale system sold to them by someone in Kampala. It works beautifully until the line drops, and then it is a very expensive paperweight with a login screen.

The assumption nobody states

Most software is designed against an implicit checklist: reliable electricity, reliable broadband, a bank card, an English-speaking administrator, and someone who can debug a sync error. In much of Uganda, one or more of those is missing on any given day.

When a product fails here, the usual explanation is "the market is not ready". That is almost always wrong. The product was not ready for the market.

What offline-first actually means

Offline-first is not a feature you bolt on. It changes the architecture: the local device is the source of truth, and the network is an optimisation. Concretely, that means three decisions.

  • Local writes always succeed. A sale, a stock count, a student payment records immediately on the device and syncs when it can.
  • Conflicts are resolved by rules, not by asking the user. A shopkeeper should never be shown a merge dialogue. The system decides, and logs what it decided.
  • The interface tells the truth about state. "Saved on this phone, waiting to sync" is honest. A spinner that never resolves is not.

Price is part of the design

The second assumption is money. If software costs more than the problem it solves, it is not a solution; it is a subscription the business will cancel in month three. Pricing for the person you claim to serve means pricing against their margin, not against a competitor's list price.

Support is the last mile

A handover that ends with a link is not a handover. Small businesses do not have an IT department, so the person who built the system has to be reachable after launch. That is not generosity; it is what makes the sale real.

The result

When you get this right, the technology disappears into the business. The owner keeps records they can trust, the numbers are available when a loan officer asks, and nobody has to explain what a sync conflict is. That is what DeryCode builds, and it is why DeryAccount was designed around the connection dropping rather than assuming it will not.

Software that needs perfect conditions is software for someone else's country.
