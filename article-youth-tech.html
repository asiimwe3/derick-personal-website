YOUTH · NOTE

You do not need
permission to build.

A note to young people in western Uganda who are waiting for a degree, a job or a title before they start.

I am twenty. I am still in school. I run several ventures in the hours that school leaves me. I am not telling you that to impress you; I am telling you because the usual advice, wait until you are qualified, is the reason a lot of good builders never start.

Nobody is coming to appoint you

There is no committee that decides when you are ready. The first client does not check your certificate; they check whether the thing works. The first customer does not care how old you are; they care whether you answered the phone.

Start with something small and real

Not a startup. A shop that needs a price list. A school that needs a fees record. A hotel that needs a page with rooms and a phone number. Small, real, finished work compounds in a way that a plan never does.

Publish the evidence

Put the work where people can see it. A live link beats a claim. When I say 59 sites are running, you can open them. That is the whole trick: make your record checkable, and the conversation about your age ends quickly.

Learn the boring parts

Payments, support, records, permissions. The interesting part of building is the idea. The durable part is the maintenance, and maintenance is where trust is earned.

Open the door behind you

When you get your first client, help someone else get theirs. That is how a region gets a technology industry instead of one lucky person.

You need a problem you care about, and the discipline to finish something small.
